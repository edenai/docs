Eden AI lets you use your own provider API keys (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, etc.) instead of going through Eden AI's billing. This gives you flexibility to leverage existing provider contracts, credits, or pricing tiers.

## Benefits

- **Use existing credits** — apply provider credits or free tiers you already have
- **Cost optimization** — take advantage of volume discounts or enterprise agreements with providers
- **Full provider access** — use provider-specific features or models that may have restricted access
- **Unified interface** — even with your own keys, you still get Eden AI's unified API, monitoring, and routing

## How to Configure

1. In the [Eden AI dashboard](https://app.edenai.run/), open **Settings > Bring Your Own Keys**. You need permission to manage your organization, and a plan that includes your own provider keys.
2. Select the provider you want to configure (for example OpenAI, Google or Anthropic). Use the search box or a category to find it.
3. Enter your provider API key and click **Save Keys**. The provider now shows as **Connected**.

Your keys apply to the whole organization. Once configured, requests routed to that provider will use your key and be billed directly by the provider.

<Note>
When using your own provider key, the `cost` field in API responses reflects the estimated cost based on the provider's public pricing. Actual billing happens on the provider's side.
</Note>

## What You Still Get

Even when using your own provider keys, Eden AI continues to provide:

- **Unified API format** — same request/response structure across all providers
- **Monitoring and logging** — track usage, latency, and errors in the Eden AI dashboard
- **Routing and fallback** — combine BYOK providers with Eden AI-billed providers in your routing logic
- **File management** — use Eden AI's persistent file storage with any provider

## When to Use BYOK

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| Getting started / prototyping | Use Eden AI billing (simpler setup) |
| Existing provider contract with volume discounts | Use BYOK |
| Need unified billing across all providers | Use Eden AI billing |
| Free tier or credits with a specific provider | Use BYOK for that provider |

<Tip>
You can mix and match — use your own key for one provider and Eden AI billing for others. This lets you optimize costs provider by provider.
</Tip>

## End-user identifiers

The LLM endpoints accept two fields that name the end user behind a request: `user`, and `safety_identifier`, which OpenAI's safety checks read. What reaches the provider depends on whose key the call runs on:

| Field | On your own provider key | On Eden AI's credentials |
|---|---|---|
| `user` | Sent to the provider as you wrote it. | Not sent as `user`: the provider receives Eden AI's identifier for your account instead. |
| `safety_identifier` | Sent to OpenAI as you wrote it. Not sent to other providers. | Not sent. On OpenAI, Eden AI's identifier for your account is sent in its place. |

This is decided for each attempt, so a fallback that runs on another provider or another key follows its own column.

Eden AI's identifier stands for your Eden AI organization and the user the API key belongs to. It is the same for all your end users, so when a provider acts on abuse seen on Eden AI's credentials, it acts on your whole Eden AI account. For a provider to tell your end users apart, add your own key for that provider.

`safety_identifier` holds at most 128 characters. A longer value is refused with `422`, or with `400` naming the line in a [batch](/v3/llms/batches), even on Eden AI's credentials, where it would not be sent.

On Eden AI's OpenAI credentials, the `safety_identifier` OpenAI echoes back is removed from the response: [Responses](/v3/llms/responses) returns it as `null`, and [image responses](/v3/llms/image-generation) leave it out. On your own key it comes back as OpenAI returned it.
